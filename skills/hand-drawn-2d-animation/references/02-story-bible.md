## 1. What the bible contains
One character sheet per character, one place sheet per location, one object sheet per hero object. These images become the references the video model uses to keep identity, one role per reference. Generate in the image tool of your choice (for example the ChatGPT plugin in CapCut), 16:9, plain paper background, no text or watermark. One board per generation; check head height and hands before the next one. Approve each sheet before moving on.

A sheet that only shows looks gives a beautiful, dead character. The sheet also says how the character moves and thinks. Fill the text fields first; the images come from the fields, not the other way round.

## 2. Character sheet: five boards

**Board 1 · Construction and proportion.** Height in heads (a 5-year-old is about 4; an adult about 6.5 to 7 in simplified drawing). Simple volumes: spheres and cylinders, no anatomy detail. Standing balance: a small child has the belly forward, knees slightly locked, feet apart. Line: soft, slightly thick pencil drawn freely, few lines per shape, never the thin uniform nervous line that makes characters stiff.

**Board 2 · Turnaround.** Front, three-quarter, profile, three-quarter back, back, on one ground line, with horizontal guide lines at top of head, eyes, chin, shoulders, waist, knees and soles. Every view hits the same lines.

**Board 3 · Hands by age, with the story objects.** Hands show age first.

| Age | Hand | Typical grip |
|---|---|---|
| 3 to 6 | short, plump, no knuckle lines, palm bigger than fingers | whole fist around objects, both hands on any cup |
| 8 to 12 | longer fingers, still rounded | thumb and forefinger pinch, both hands for heavy things |
| adult | knuckles, narrow palm | finger grip, one hand, economical gestures |

Draw six hand poses with the real objects of the story. The finger count is part of the model and repeats in every pose.

**Board 4 · States with a cause, not generic emotions.** At least eight, each with its cause in parentheses: attentive (listening to someone), curious (leaning in to sniff something), pleasure of eating (cheeks full, eyes closing), surprise (eyebrows rise before the mouth opens), whole-body laugh, a child's cry (scrunched face, square mouth, no pretty tears), sleep winning, embarrassment (eyes slide sideways, shoulders up). Eyebrows carry most of the change; eye shape changes little; full cheeks change the face outline; one white highlight per eye, always on the same side.

**Board 5 · Acting repertoire.** Short sentences that go literally into the acting paragraph of prompts: main want; how they walk; how they run (a small child runs with legs apart and arms out, and falls); how they eat; one habit with a trigger; one physical mistake; how they listen; what they copy from others; one contradiction.

## 3. Colour sheet
| Area | Base | Line colour | Shadow |
|---|---|---|---|
| skin | | warm brown, never black | none, only a soft cheek blush |
| hair | | darker tone of the hair | |
| clothing | | darker tone of each piece | |

Flat colour on characters, no cel shadow on the face, line coloured per area. Light and weather live in the background and in effects, never painted on the character. The identity colour never changes, not at night, not under water. Night or rain only cools the secondary colours.

## 4. Fixed prompt blocks
Write them once and paste them into every board prompt. Describe technique, never a film, studio or director.

```text
STYLE
Clean hand-drawn 2D animation model sheet on plain warm off-white paper. Soft, slightly thick pencil outline drawn freely by hand, with natural pressure variation and few lines per shape, in warm dark brown, never pure black, never thin and mechanical; outline colour shifts per area. Flat cel colours with no cel shading on skin or face, only a soft pink blush on the cheeks. Simple round construction, child proportions, readable silhouettes. Small neat handwritten-style labels only where asked, no other text, no watermark.
```

```text
CHARACTER (example)
A boy of about eight, about 4.5 heads tall: dark-brown curly hair, round thin black glasses, plum beret with two blue feathers on its left side, plum short-sleeve shirt with small gold buttons over a lavender undershirt, brown belt, navy shorts, slouchy brown boots. Soft rounded child hands with short fingers, exactly five fingers on each hand (four fingers plus one thumb), palms wider than the fingers are long.
```

## 5. Board prompts
Each one is `[STYLE] + [CHARACTER] + the board text`.

- **P1 Turnaround:** "Turnaround of the same boy in five views on one ground line: front, three-quarter front, profile, three-quarter back, back. Relaxed neutral pose, belly slightly forward, knees soft, feet a little apart. Thin horizontal guide lines at top of head, eye line, chin, shoulders, waist, knees and soles; every view hits the same lines."
- **P2 Hands:** "Hand study sheet, six drawings in two rows: holding a small wrapped gum piece with both hands; tearing a wrapper with a thumb-and-forefinger pinch; gripping a spoon in a full fist; holding a mug with both hands; open palm offered upward; hand resting flat on a table edge. Child hands, short rounded fingers, no knuckle lines. Exactly five fingers (four fingers plus one thumb) on every hand in every drawing, never four, never six."
- **P3 States:** the eight states of board 4, each with its cause, head and shoulders, two rows.
- **P4 Acting sequence:** one everyday action in eight poses left to right with small circled numbers (for example: notices, kneels and reaches, checks the object close to the face, tears the wrapper, pops it in, first chew with eyes closing, eyes snap open as an idea arrives, deep breath ready to blow).
- **P5 Colour model:** front view twice, day and night palettes; only secondary colours cool down at night; swatch column with the outline colour of each area next to its fill.
- **P6 Cel breakdown:** the same three-quarter view as separate layers (body without face, face features, hair and feathers, composite), plus six mouth shapes and six eye sets aligned to registration marks.

## 6. Place and object sheets
Place: the geography (what is left, right, behind), the path the character takes, the light direction, a foreground layer (darker, more contrast), a soft distant layer. Painted, selective detail, soft contrast in the distance.
Object: the hero object from several angles with its exact design and label. The packaging never changes design, so it gets its own reference. Objects are landmarks: they stay in the same place from every angle.

## 7. Originality check
Before approving a character, ask: does it read as a famous character? BOLHAO's second version (green tunic, feathered cap) did, even with no name in the prompt; a video job with that sheet failed with credits returned, and the plugin chat blamed copyright (never confirmed). The fix was a new costume of its own (plum beret, two blue feathers, plum shirt) on the same face. A colour swap or a missing name is not a licence. A real person's likeness (here, the creator as a child) needs that person's consent, and the age in every sheet must match the story: an "adult waking up" frame had to be redone as a child.

## 8. Checklist
- [ ] Reads as its own character, not as a famous one
- [ ] Same head height in all five turnaround views
- [ ] Hands match the age and hold the story objects, five fingers in every drawing
- [ ] Eight states, each with a written cause
- [ ] Acting repertoire filled, with at least one physical mistake and one imitation
- [ ] Colour sheet with a line colour per area
- [ ] Clean stills: no stamp, no text inside the image, never a frame taken from a generated video
